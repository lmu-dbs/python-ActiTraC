What ran where

ActiTraC-ProM itself was never run here: its numbers are the published results of Peeperkorn et al., and the emulation reruns its algorithm in plain Java, in Docker. "Clusters only" means actitraceR's labels were saved before its final evaluation, which needs about 12 hours on Sepsis. "Run, refined" means the log's lifecycle values were changed so actitraceR keeps any events, the same input went to the replica and python-ActiTraC, and on BPIC13 empty clusters were dropped instead of stopping with an error. BPIC12 and BPIC15 needed a second batch with a 48-hour limit, after 15 runs reached the 12-hour limit; every run then finished.

Run time, in seconds

Helpdesk and Sepsis ran 16 jobs at once on 16 CPUs, the other logs 32 on 32. "Rerun 15" marks rows with runs that reached the 12-hour limit and ran again, 15 at once on 16 CPUs with a 48-hour limit, so the load differed. actitraceR's clustering stops where its final evaluation starts, and its total includes that evaluation, which was left out on the other logs. The replica and python-ActiTraC leave the evaluation out. On BPIC12 and BPIC15 python-ActiTraC, in its Inductive Miner setup, is far slower than the replica; the likely cause is that it keeps growing clusters to the minimal size counted at the start and aligns every remaining variant in its look-ahead, but that has not been measured.

How far to trust these numbers

  • ActiTraC-ProM's cluster logs aren't published, so membership against ActiTraC-ProM is indirect. ER, graph density and graph entropy depend only on membership.
  • Metrics that use pm4py's Inductive Miner vary between runs on identical clusters, so small differences in them mean little.
  • The rows for ActiTraC-ProM and for python-ActiTraC's default and prom_compatible settings come from the benchmark run with unset hash seeds. The Inductive Miner setup rows were scored afterwards on hash seed 0, with the same pm4py version (2.7.8.4). The seed in those rows is the clustering seed.
  • actitraceR ran on five settings on Helpdesk, two extra windows there, and two settings on Sepsis, and on targets 1.00 and 0.99 on the other six logs. It keeps only events whose lifecycle is exactly complete, so on raw BPIC13 and BPIC12 it finds 0 traces. For those three logs BPIC12's COMPLETE became lower case and every BPIC13 event was marked complete, for all three implementations. On BPIC13, actitraceR's empty clusters were dropped, because they stop it with an error; the original error logs are kept.
  • Of the 72 runs on the six other logs, 15 reached the 12-hour limit, all on BPIC12 and BPIC15. Rerun with a 48-hour limit, all 15 finished, in about 12 to 27 hours. python-ActiTraC on BPIC15 at target 0.99, seed 1, took the longest, about 97,600 s; actitraceR there took 84,797 s.
  • The Inductive Miner setup rows on the other six logs were scored the same way, on all events of the log as in the paper, though actitraceR clustered only the complete events. BPIC12 and BPIC15 skip the alignment metrics, also as in the paper.
  • actitraceR, the replica and python-ActiTraC's Inductive Miner setup ran on pm4py 2.7.2, the version actitraceR's wrapper pins. The clusters in each comparison were made on that version.
